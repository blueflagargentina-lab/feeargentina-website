## New EU Rules on Green Claims Affect Certification Timing

The Foundation for Environmental Education (FEE), which runs the Blue Flag Programme, has announced transitional arrangements for certified sites in the European Union as Directive (EU) 2024/825 on empowering consumers for the green transition comes into force. EU member states were required to transpose the directive into national law by 27 March 2026, with the corresponding measures applying from 27 September 2026 onward. The directive tightens rules around environmental claims and sustainability labelling used in commercial communications, prompting Blue Flag to review how the certification is displayed and communicated across its European network.

## Most Sites Unaffected, but Some Require Guidance

According to FEE, the large majority of Blue Flag beaches and marinas in the EU will have already completed their 2026 season before the new rules take effect on 27 September, meaning the flag is no longer flying at those locations under normal seasonal operations. The transitional measures are therefore aimed at a smaller group of sites with extended or year-round seasons that continue past that date, including some privately managed beaches, marinas and tourism boats where commercial use of the Blue Flag label could fall under the EU's Unfair Commercial Practices Directive, depending on the specific circumstances.

## Commission Clarification Shapes the Approach

FEE says it received clarification from the European Commission in September 2026 in response to a parliamentary question on the Blue Flag Programme. The Commission indicated that recognition of a publicly managed beach does not automatically amount to a business-to-consumer commercial practice, while use of the Blue Flag by a trader as part of marketing communications may fall within the scope of the consumer-protection framework, depending on context. Based on this guidance, FEE is coordinating with national Blue Flag Operators to apply a precautionary and proportionate approach for the limited number of sites whose 2026 season extends beyond 27 September.

## Transitional Measures and Next Steps

For private and commercial sites affected by the new timeline, FEE plans to offer an additional independent verification option, allowing eligible operators to undergo a third-party desktop audit of their existing 2026 Blue Flag application. Sites that receive confirmation through their National Operator may continue displaying the flag for the remainder of their operating season; where confirmation has not been finalised, FEE's precautionary guidance is for sites to stop publicly displaying the flag by the end of 26 September 2026. FEE has stressed that the Blue Flag Programme continues to operate and is reinforcing its governance and verification framework to align with the evolving EU regulatory landscape, with further details on the recognition model for future seasons expected to be shared with the Blue Flag network as the framework is finalised.
