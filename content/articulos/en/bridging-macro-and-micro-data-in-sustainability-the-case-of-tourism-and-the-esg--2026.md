## A New Push for Standardized Sustainability Data

During the 57th session of the UN Statistical Commission in New York, UN Tourism joined forces with the United Nations Committee of Experts on Business and Trade Statistics, Statistics Netherlands, and the Permanent Mission of the Kingdom of the Netherlands to the United Nations to present a coordinated Environmental, Social, and Governance (ESG) Framework for tourism businesses. The initiative seeks to align sustainability reporting across the tourism sector with internationally recognized statistical standards, closing the gap between broad national-level data and the operational realities faced by individual companies.

## Linking National Statistics to Business-Level Reporting

The session, moderated by national statistical leaders and attended by private sector representatives from major travel and hospitality companies, focused on how National Statistical Offices and international organizations can collaborate to produce comparable, policy-relevant sustainability data. Officials from UN Tourism's Statistics, Standards and Data division emphasized that reliable ESG metrics are increasingly essential for destinations—including coastal areas and marinas—seeking to demonstrate credible environmental performance to travelers, investors, and certification bodies.

## Relevance for Coastal and Blue Flag Destinations

Although the framework targets the tourism industry broadly, its emphasis on harmonized environmental and social indicators carries particular significance for beach and marina operators pursuing eco-certifications such as Blue Flag. Consistent ESG data could help local authorities and businesses better document water quality management, waste reduction, and biodiversity protection efforts, reinforcing the credibility of sustainability labels used in coastal tourism.

## Looking Ahead to 2026

With the framework still under development, stakeholders from national statistics agencies, private companies, and international bodies are expected to continue refining methodologies throughout 2026. The goal is to establish a globally consistent reporting structure that tourism businesses—ranging from large travel platforms to small coastal operators—can adopt to measure and communicate their environmental and social impact more transparently.
