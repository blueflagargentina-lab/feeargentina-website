## Modest Growth Reflects a Fragile Global Recovery

International tourism expanded by only 0.4% during the first half of 2026, according to new data released by UN Tourism. The figure signals a marked slowdown compared to previous post-pandemic recovery years, as the sector contends with instability in the Middle East, elevated oil prices, and persistent inflationary pressures worldwide.

## Coastal and Marine Destinations Feel the Impact

While the report covers tourism broadly, coastal and marine destinations—many of them holders of Blue Flag certification—are among the segments most sensitive to shifts in travel costs and geopolitical uncertainty. Beach resorts, marinas, and island economies often depend heavily on international visitor spending, meaning that even small fluctuations in global arrivals can translate into measurable effects on local tourism revenue and environmental management budgets.

## Sustainability as a Resilience Strategy

Industry observers note that destinations maintaining strong environmental credentials, such as those certified under the Blue Flag programme, may be better positioned to weather periods of slower growth. Sustainable practices—ranging from water quality monitoring to responsible waste management—can enhance a destination's long-term appeal, helping maintain visitor trust even as broader economic and geopolitical headwinds persist through 2026.

## Outlook for the Remainder of the Year

UN Tourism cautioned that the pace of recovery for the rest of 2026 will depend largely on how global energy prices and regional conflicts evolve. For coastal tourism stakeholders, the current climate underscores the importance of diversifying visitor markets and continuing investments in sustainability certifications that reinforce destination resilience amid ongoing global uncertainty.
