## Tourism Rebound Raises Stakes for Coastal Management

The World Tourism Organization (UN Tourism) has confirmed that international tourist arrivals exceeded 1.5 billion in 2025, marking one of the strongest years on record for the global travel sector. The figures were highlighted during the organization's February 2026 update to its network of affiliate members, which also noted that FITUR 2026, held in Madrid, drew more than 255,000 visitors across five days, reinforcing the sector's continued momentum heading into the new year.

## Coastal Destinations Face Growing Visitor Pressure

As international travel volumes climb, coastal regions — among the most popular destinations for leisure tourism — are expected to experience heightened pressure on natural resources, water quality, and marine ecosystems. Programs such as Blue Flag, which certifies beaches and marinas meeting strict environmental, safety, and accessibility criteria, are increasingly viewed as essential tools for destinations seeking to balance rising visitor numbers with long-term environmental protection in 2026 and beyond.

## New Initiatives Support Sustainable Tourism Development

UN Tourism also highlighted recent collaborative efforts among its affiliate members, including the publication of a guide for developing astrotourism experiences, produced with the Starlight Foundation. While focused on stargazing tourism, the initiative reflects a broader industry trend toward niche, low-impact tourism models that complement traditional beach and marina destinations already pursuing eco-certifications. Many coastal municipalities are expected to draw on similar frameworks to diversify offerings while protecting fragile shoreline environments.

## Digital Tools Aim to Strengthen Industry Collaboration

The organization reported strong engagement with its AMConnected+ platform, now available via a mobile app on both iOS and Android, which allows tourism stakeholders to share resources and explore partnership opportunities. For coastal destinations pursuing or maintaining Blue Flag status, such digital collaboration tools could prove valuable in coordinating sustainability efforts across public and private stakeholders as global tourism continues its post-pandemic expansion into 2026.
