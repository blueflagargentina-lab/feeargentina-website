## A Global Gathering on the Future of Sports Tourism

Mexico City will host the 4th World Sports Tourism Congress from 7 to 9 September 2026, organized by UN Tourism in partnership with Mexico's federal and city tourism authorities, the Mexico City Tourism Promotion Fund, and the Confederation of National Chambers of Commerce, Services and Tourism (CONCANACO SERVYTUR). Taking place shortly after the FIFA World Cup 2026, for which Mexico serves as a co-host nation, the event will convene government officials, destination managers, sports federations and private-sector leaders to discuss how sports tourism can drive investment, infrastructure development and community benefit.

## Why Coastal Destinations Should Pay Attention

Among the congress agenda items are sustainability, inclusion and accessibility in sports tourism, along with destination branding and territorial transformation—topics with direct relevance to coastal regions that host water-based sporting events such as sailing regattas, triathlons and open-water swimming competitions. For beaches and marinas already holding Blue Flag certification, the discussions could offer a framework for aligning sports event planning with established environmental quality standards, reinforcing the link between eco-certification and destination appeal.

## A Fast-Growing Segment of Global Tourism

Sports tourism is estimated to represent around 10% of global tourism spending, reaching an approximate value of USD 672 billion in 2025. Organizers note that the sector has broadened beyond large-scale events to include active and outdoor tourism, amateur competitions and community-based experiences, extending its economic reach to emerging and rural destinations. This shift increases the relevance of sustainable infrastructure—such as certified beaches and marinas—as host communities seek to balance visitor growth with environmental protection.

## Looking Ahead to 2026 and Beyond

As host destinations prepare for a packed international sporting calendar culminating in 2026, coastal municipalities with Blue Flag-certified sites may find opportunities to position themselves within the sports tourism value chain, leveraging recognized environmental standards as a competitive advantage. The congress's sessions on governance, investment and public-private collaboration could further inform how local authorities integrate sustainability criteria into future sports tourism strategies.
