## A Flag Restored After Two Decades

Duncannon Beach, located on Ireland's southeast coast, has officially reclaimed its Blue Flag status in 2026, marking the first time the certification has flown over the beach in 19 years. The announcement was celebrated by local residents who gathered despite poor weather to witness the flag-raising ceremony, led by longtime community member Walter Foley.

## A Legacy of Community Stewardship

Duncannon's relationship with the Blue Flag programme dates back to 1988, just a year after the international certification was established. At the time, Walter's father, John Foley, had already built a tradition of organizing weekly beach cleanups that engaged local schoolchildren and residents, often rewarding volunteers with ice cream for their efforts. The beach's original award was seen as a proud milestone for the small coastal community.

## Overcoming Water Quality Challenges

Over the following decades, Duncannon Beach faced persistent water quality issues that prevented it from maintaining Blue Flag standards. Local and regional authorities responded with targeted infrastructure investments, including a new sewage treatment system, alongside an EU-funded initiative designed to help farmers in the area reduce agricultural run-off into nearby streams and rivers. These combined efforts gradually improved coastal water quality to the point where the beach could once again meet the programme's strict environmental and safety criteria.

## A Multigenerational Celebration

The 2026 ceremony brought together descendants of the original 1988 committee, including family members standing in for those no longer able to attend, creating a symbolic bridge between generations of environmental stewardship. Walter Foley described the moment as deeply meaningful, crediting decades of commitment from community groups, Tidy Towns volunteers, agricultural initiatives, and local infrastructure upgrades. The return of the Blue Flag to Duncannon Beach stands as a reminder of how sustained local action can reverse environmental setbacks and restore recognition for coastal communities.
