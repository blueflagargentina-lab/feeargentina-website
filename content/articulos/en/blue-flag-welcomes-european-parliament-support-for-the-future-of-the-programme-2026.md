## Cross-Party Support in the European Parliament

A coalition of thirty Members of the European Parliament (MEPs) has formally asked the European Commission to ensure the long-term viability of the Blue Flag Programme, one of the world's most recognised eco-certifications for beaches, marinas and tourism boats. The initiative, spearheaded by Greek MEP and S&D Group Vice-Chair Yannis Maniatis, follows concerns raised by the Foundation for Environmental Education (FEE), which manages the Blue Flag scheme globally.

## Concerns Over New EU Legislation

At the center of the issue is Directive (EU) 2024/825, known as the Empowering Consumers for the Green Transition (EmpCo) Directive, designed to tighten rules around environmental claims on consumer products. According to the MEPs' letter and parliamentary question submitted to the Commission, applying this consumer-focused legislation to public environmental programmes like Blue Flag could impose disproportionate administrative and financial burdens on municipalities that manage certified sites, potentially discouraging participation.

## A Longstanding Environmental Benchmark

Since its founding in 1987, Blue Flag has grown into a globally trusted symbol of environmental performance, currently active in 52 countries. In the European Union alone, roughly 3,800 beaches, marinas and tourism boats across 22 member states carry the certification, based on strict criteria covering water quality, environmental management, safety, accessibility and environmental education. Supporters argue the programme plays a direct role in advancing EU policy goals on bathing water quality and sustainable coastal tourism, making its protection a matter of public interest as the bloc moves toward its environmental targets for 2026 and beyond.

## FEE Calls for Continued Dialogue

In a written statement, Maniatis described Blue Flag as "an extremely successful environmental initiative" that functions as more than a quality symbol, calling on the Commission to prevent the new directive from creating unjustified obstacles to a programme he considers a European success story. Johann Durand, International Blue Flag Director at FEE, welcomed the parliamentary support, noting that the organisation shares the goal of strengthening consumer trust through credible certification and is committed to working with the Commission to align implementation of the EmpCo Directive with the continuity of established environmental programmes. FEE said it will keep engaging with EU institutions, national operators and coastal communities to preserve Blue Flag's role in promoting cleaner beaches and sustainable tourism across Europe.
